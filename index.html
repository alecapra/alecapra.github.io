Hi, welcome to my page.

I am a PhD candidate in Economics at the University of Amsterdam. My supervisors are Sander Onderstal and J.W. Stoelhorst.

My research uses experimental methods to study the interplay between personal and social norms in social dilemmas, and how behavioural mechanism design can harness individual preferences to achieve welfare improvements. I am also interested in how the allocation of advantages and disadvantages shapes our perceptions of ourselves and others, and our interactions with them.

You can reach me at a.capra@uva.nl.

Pre-PhD Work

Leader Legitimacy in Social Dilemmas: How Do Different Ways of Appointing Leaders Shape Cooperation in Public Good Games?
Supervised by S. Onderstal and J.W. Stoelhorst
ThemeDetail
Research question Does the way leaders are appointed affect cooperation in public good games?
Motivation Standard results show first movers can increase contributions, but effects are inconsistent. With partial reciprocity, perceived leader legitimacy may play a key role.
Design One-shot sequential public good game. Leader appointed via random selection, benevolence-based selection (dictator game donations), or competence-based selection (control question performance).
Key findings Non-random selection affects contributions but not through perceived legitimacy. Competent leaders contribute more on average. Benevolent leader selection leads to lower contributions from both movers.
Mechanism First movers anticipate lower reciprocity from less charitable second movers; second movers expect higher contributions from benevolent leaders. A mismatch between expectations and actual contributions drives reduced cooperation.
Implication Leader selection rules matter, but their effects operate through beliefs and expectation gaps, not legitimacy per se.
